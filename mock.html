Problem 1 · Computational graph (10 points) \[ f(x, y) = (x + y)\cdot \min\!\left(x \cdot y,\; \frac{y}{x}\right), \qquad x = 2,\; y = 6 \]

Draw the computational graph. Show all forward values and the backward partial derivative at each node. Compute the numerical values of \(\partial f/\partial x\) and \(\partial f/\partial y\).

Solution and marking

Forward. \(a = x + y = 8\); \(b = x\cdot y = 12\); \(c = y/x = 3\); \(d = \min(12, 3) = 3\) (c is selected); \(f = a \cdot d = 24\).

Backward. \(\partial f/\partial a = d = 3\); \(\partial f/\partial d = a = 8\). The min sends 8 to \(c\) and 0 to \(b\).

PathLocal derivative× upstreamValue
x through \(a = x + y\)1× 33
y through \(a\)1× 33
x, y through \(b = x\cdot y\)y, x× 00
x through \(c = y/x\)\(-y/x^2 = -1.5\)× 8−12
y through \(c\)\(1/x = 0.5\)× 84
\[ \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} = 3 + 0 - 12 = \mathbf{-9} \qquad \frac{\partial f}{\partial y} = 3 + 0 + 4 = \mathbf{7} \]

Check. The active branch is \(f = (x + y)\dfrac{y}{x} = y + \dfrac{y^2}{x}\). So \(\partial f/\partial x = -y^2/x^2 = -9\) and \(\partial f/\partial y = 1 + 2y/x = 7\).

Problem 3 · Softmax and cross-entropy (6 points)

A network with two nodes in its last layer produces the output \(\begin{bmatrix}2\\0\end{bmatrix}\) for some input. The desired output is \(\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}\).

(a) Apply softmax. (b) Compute the cross-entropy loss using the natural log. (c) What would the loss be if the desired output were \(\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}\) instead, and what does the comparison tell you?

Solution and marking

(a) \(e^2 = 7.389\), \(e^0 = 1\), sum \(= 8.389\). \(\hat{y} = [0.881,\; 0.119]\).

(b) \(L = -\ln(0.119) =\) 2.127. Shortcut: \(\ln\hat{y}_2 = 0 - \ln 8.389 = -2.127\).

(c) \(L = -\ln(0.881) =\) 0.127. The same output costs about 17 times more when it is wrong: cross-entropy punishes confident wrong predictions heavily and rewards confident correct ones.

  • Softmax gives [0.881, 0.119]2
  • Loss is 2.127 (about 2.13)2
  • Loss 0.127 for the other target, with the explanation2
Bonus · Convolution and pooling by hand (4 points)

Using the 4×4 input and the 2×2 filter below (no padding, slide one step at a time, no flipping):

\[ g = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 & 1 & 3 \\ 4 & 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 1 & 5 & 2 & 2 \\ 0 & 3 & 6 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \qquad f = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \]

(a) Compute the activation map \(f * g\). (b) Apply 2×2 max pooling to the original input \(g\).

Solution and marking

(a) Output size 4 − 2 + 1 = 3. With this filter each output is (top-left) − (bottom-left) of the patch, i.e. each entry minus the one below it.

\[ f * g = \begin{bmatrix} 2-4 & 0-1 & 1-0 \\ 4-1 & 1-5 & 0-2 \\ 1-0 & 5-3 & 2-6 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -2 & -1 & 1 \\ 3 & -4 & -2 \\ 1 & 2 & -4 \end{bmatrix} \]

(b) Blocks {2, 0, 4, 1}, {1, 3, 0, 2}, {1, 5, 0, 3}, {2, 2, 6, 1}:

\[ \text{max pool} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 & 3 \\ 5 & 6 \end{bmatrix} \]
  • 3×3 activation map correct2
  • 2×2 max-pooled output correct2
